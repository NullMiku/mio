# Mio Code of Conduct

Mio welcomes people with different backgrounds and levels of experience. Reporting a bug, asking a question, improving documentation, adding a test, and contributing code are all valuable ways to participate.

We do not expect everyone to agree on technical choices. We do expect discussions to remain focused on ideas and their consequences, rather than personal judgments.

## Expected behavior

- Critique code, designs, benchmarks, and decisions directly and constructively. Explain the reasoning or provide evidence whenever possible.
- Respect people who are learning or unfamiliar with the project. Basic questions are welcome.
- Respect other contributors' time. Do not pressure anyone with repeated demands for immediate attention.
- Be willing to clarify misunderstandings and consider different technical approaches.
- Treat contributors and maintainers with the same basic courtesy.

Disagreement, a negative review, or a decision not to merge a PR is not a Code of Conduct violation by itself.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, personal attacks, or discriminatory remarks.
- Unwanted sexual attention or sexual harassment.
- Publishing someone else's private information without permission.
- Impersonation, malicious disruption, spam, or continued unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Retaliation against someone for making a good-faith report.

These expectations also apply to maintainers.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to Mio's Issues, Pull Requests, Discussions, and communication spaces explicitly designated as official project channels. It is not intended to police unrelated private lives or personal opinions.

## Reporting and enforcement

For conduct concerns that can safely be discussed publicly, you may open an Issue in the repository. **Do not publish private information or sensitive incident details in a public Issue.**

For sensitive reports directed to the maintainers, use the project's private community contact once it is published:

GitHub's own abuse-reporting tools remain available for violations of GitHub's platform rules. GitHub Private Vulnerability Reporting is reserved for software security issues, not community conduct complaints.

Maintainers will consider the available facts and, when appropriate, contact the people involved. They will limit the sharing of sensitive reports to what is reasonably needed for handling the situation, but cannot promise absolute confidentiality.

Depending on the severity and circumstances, actions may include a reminder, warning, removal of inappropriate content, restrictions on participation, or a ban from project spaces. Reports involving a maintainer should be handled by an uninvolved maintainer where possible; if that is not possible, the conflict should be disclosed to the reporter.

Contribution workflow, testing, and PR standards are described separately in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
